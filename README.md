# REWIRE

### Turning Fraud Exposure Into Fraud Prevention, Skills & Opportunity.

> **What if the people who understand fraud best could become the people who stop it?**

REWIRE is an **anti-fraud EdTech and opportunity platform designed for Nigeria**.

The platform adapts the proven structure of Hong Kong's public anti-scam education model—**real-world scam cases, educational content, interactive quizzes, practical scenarios, and certification**—and extends it into a new direction:

**Teach → Simulate → Prove → Rewire → Earn.**

Rather than treating fraud only as a law-enforcement problem, REWIRE addresses one of the underlying drivers of digital fraud among young people: **the absence of accessible, credible pathways from digital skills and fraud exposure into legitimate economic opportunity.**

REWIRE gives users a structured way to understand how fraud works, recognize and prevent it, demonstrate fraud-prevention skills, earn verified credentials, and eventually connect those skills to legitimate work.

---

# 1. The Problem

Nigeria has a large, highly connected young population, a rapidly growing digital economy, and increasing dependence on online financial services.

At the same time, digital fraud continues to damage individuals, businesses, financial institutions, and trust in online commerce.

The conventional response tends to focus on the **victim side**:

> Teach people how not to get scammed.

That is necessary—but incomplete.

There is another side of the problem.

Some young people are exposed to online fraud communities because those communities appear to offer:

* fast income,
* accessible digital skills,
* status within peer networks,
* and a perceived alternative to limited legitimate opportunities.

This creates an incentive problem.

Telling someone:

> "Don't commit fraud."

does not necessarily answer:

> **"Then what can I do with these digital skills to make money legitimately?"**

REWIRE tackles this gap.

The platform does not glorify fraud or teach users how to commit it.

Instead, it takes the **knowledge, curiosity, and analytical abilities surrounding fraud** and redirects them toward:

* fraud awareness,
* fraud detection,
* cybersecurity fundamentals,
* digital safety,
* critical thinking,
* scam analysis,
* and legitimate digital work.

The objective is simple:

> **Replace the economic pathway toward fraud with a pathway toward legitimate opportunity.**

---

# 2. The Core Insight

The central idea behind REWIRE is:

## Don't only teach people how to avoid fraud. Teach people how to understand and prevent it.

Fraud prevention becomes more effective when people understand:

* how manipulation works,
* how common scams are structured,
* what warning signs look like,
* why victims respond to certain tactics,
* how digital deception spreads,
* how suspicious activity can be analyzed,
* and what preventative action should be taken.

The people who are naturally interested in these mechanisms can become a valuable source of legitimate anti-fraud talent.

REWIRE therefore creates a bridge:

```text
Fraud Exposure
      ↓
Structured Education
      ↓
Practical Simulation
      ↓
Skill Verification
      ↓
Credential
      ↓
Legitimate Opportunity
      ↓
Fraud Prevention
```

This creates a positive feedback loop.

More trained users → more fraud-awareness capacity → more legitimate opportunities → stronger incentives to remain on the legal side of the digital economy.

# 3. Inspiration: The Hong Kong Anti-Scam Education Model

A major foundation of REWIRE is inspired by the **Anti-Deception Coordination Centre (ADCC)** of the Hong Kong Police Force.

Hong Kong's approach demonstrates that fighting fraud is not limited to investigations and enforcement. **Public education, practical awareness, training, and continuous communication can be integrated into a broader anti-deception strategy.**

The Hong Kong ADCC operates as a centralized anti-deception initiative within the Hong Kong Police Force. Its functions include public anti-scam education, coordination, scam trend monitoring, risk assessment, and support for anti-deception efforts.

One particularly relevant component is the **Anti-Scam Online Learning Package**.

The package developed by the Hong Kong Police Force's Anti-Deception Coordination Centre includes:

* anti-scam videos,
* real-life case studies,
* quizzes,
* e-certificates,
* explanations of prevalent scam tactics,
* and practical prevention advice.

Its subject areas include telephone scams, investment scams, shopping scams, romance scams, and job scams. Users can receive an e-certificate after achieving the required quiz score.

This is the part of the Hong Kong model REWIRE directly adopts:

```text
Real Cases
   ↓
Education
   ↓
Interactive Assessment
   ↓
Knowledge Verification
   ↓
Certification
```

REWIRE then asks a different question:

> **What happens after the user becomes good at this?**

That is where REWIRE extends the model.

Instead of certification being the endpoint, REWIRE makes certification the beginning of a **legitimate skills pathway**.

```text
Hong Kong Model

Cases → Learning → Quiz → Certificate
                    ↓
             Fraud Awareness


REWIRE

Cases → Learning → Simulation → Assessment
                    ↓
               Skill Profile
                    ↓
                Certificate
                    ↓
             Opportunity Pathway
                    ↓
             Legitimate Work
                    ↓
             Fraud Prevention
```

This distinction is central to the product.

# 4. Why Hong Kong's Model Matters

The significance of the Hong Kong approach is not that REWIRE is copying a government website.

The significance is the **methodology**.

The ADCC already demonstrates an institutional model where anti-fraud education is made:

**practical + scenario-based + interactive + measurable.**

The ADCC's current ecosystem also extends beyond a single online course, incorporating anti-scam videos, university activities, educational campaigns, and other outreach programs.

For REWIRE, this provides a valuable design principle:

> **Fraud education should not feel like a textbook.**

It should feel like a practical skill.

A learner should encounter situations such as:

> "You receive this message. What do you notice?"

> "This transaction has these characteristics. What should you investigate?"

> "This job offer contains these signals. Which parts are suspicious?"

> "A person is applying psychological pressure. What tactic might be involved?"

The learner makes a decision.

The platform explains the reasoning.

The learner tries again.

Their performance becomes measurable.

This transforms passive awareness into **demonstrated competency**.

# 5. The REWIRE Solution

REWIRE is an **interactive anti-fraud learning platform** built around three connected layers.

## Layer 1 — Learn

Users receive short, practical educational content based on real-world fraud patterns.

Instead of long academic lectures, REWIRE uses:

* short lessons,
* case studies,
* visual explanations,
* interactive examples,
* quizzes,
* scenario-based questions.

The curriculum is localized around the Nigerian digital environment.

Examples include:

* online marketplace scams,
* fake investment opportunities,
* social engineering,
* impersonation,
* phishing,
* fake employment opportunities,
* account takeover risks,
* fraudulent payment requests,
* digital identity abuse,
* and common online financial deception patterns.

The objective is not to teach users how to execute these attacks.

The objective is to teach them how to **recognize, analyze, and prevent them**.

---

# 6. Layer 2 — Simulate

This is where REWIRE moves beyond conventional EdTech.

Instead of asking:

> "Did you watch the lesson?"

REWIRE asks:

> **"Can you actually recognize the problem?"**

Users enter simulated scenarios.

For example:

```text
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
       SCENARIO #07
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A user receives a message claiming
their financial account will be suspended
unless they verify their information
through an attached link.

What should the user do?

A. Click immediately
B. Reply to the sender
C. Verify through an official channel
D. Forward the message to friends

             [ SUBMIT ]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

The system evaluates the decision.

The learner receives feedback explaining:

* what they identified correctly,
* what signals they missed,
* why the decision matters,
* and how to recognize similar patterns in the future.

The same methodology can be applied to more advanced scenarios.

# 7. Layer 3 — Prove

Completion is not enough.

REWIRE creates a **Fraud Prevention Skill Profile** based on demonstrated performance.

For example:

```text
REWIRE SKILL PROFILE

Fraud Awareness        █████████░  92%
Social Engineering     ████████░░  81%
Digital Safety         █████████░  89%
Scam Detection         █████████░  91%
Critical Analysis      ████████░░  84%

Overall Score: 88%

STATUS:
Certified Fraud Prevention Learner
```

This produces a meaningful distinction:

### Course completion ≠ competency.

A user should earn recognition by demonstrating that they understand the material.

The same philosophy underlies the Hong Kong learning model's use of quizzes and e-certification. REWIRE builds on that structure by making the learner's demonstrated ability a reusable profile.

# 9. Learn → Earn

This is the defining extension of REWIRE.

The platform should not stop at:

> "Congratulations, you completed the course."

The learner should be able to ask:

> **"What can I do with what I just learned?"**

REWIRE therefore introduces an opportunity layer.

Users who demonstrate sufficient competency can become eligible for legitimate opportunities related to:

* fraud-awareness programs,
* digital safety education,
* cybersecurity learning pathways,
* scam reporting and review workflows,
* research assistance,
* moderation and trust-and-safety tasks,
* internships,
* community digital-safety programs,
* and other verified digital roles.

The platform can eventually connect certified learners with organizations looking for people with relevant skills.

This creates the economic incentive that makes the model much more powerful.

# 12. Why Nigeria?

Nigeria is an ideal environment for this problem because the country has:

* a large and youthful population,
* rapid adoption of digital financial services,
* a growing technology ecosystem,
* significant online economic activity,
* and a large population of young people seeking meaningful digital opportunities.

The opportunity is therefore not simply to build another anti-scam awareness website.

The opportunity is to build a **digital pathway that connects fraud prevention, education, skills, and economic opportunity.**

Nigeria also provides an unusually important environment for localized anti-fraud education.

A globally generic cybersecurity course may teach:

> "Beware of suspicious emails."

REWIRE can teach:

> "Here is a realistic situation you are likely to encounter in the Nigerian digital environment. What do you do next?"

That localization is essential.

# 13. Product Principles

REWIRE is built around five principles.

## 1. Practical over theoretical

Users should make decisions, not just watch videos.

## 2. Prevention over fear

The product teaches people to recognize risk rather than simply frightening them about scams.

## 3. Skills over completion

Certificates should reflect demonstrated competence.

## 4. Opportunity over punishment

The platform should make legitimate digital skills economically meaningful.

## 5. Safety by design

REWIRE teaches **fraud detection and prevention**, not operational instructions for committing fraud.

# 14. MVP

The hackathon MVP should stay deliberately narrow.

We do **not** need to build the entire REWIRE ecosystem.

The MVP should prove one complete user journey:

> **A learner discovers REWIRE → completes a Nigerian anti-fraud lesson → faces realistic scenarios → demonstrates competence → receives a skill score/certificate → sees a legitimate opportunity pathway.**

### MVP Components

## 1. Landing Page

Explain:

* the problem,
* the REWIRE concept,
* why the approach matters,
* and the Learn → Prove → Earn pathway.

---

## 2. User Authentication

Basic:

* registration,
* login,
* learner profile.

---

## 3. Learning Module

Start with approximately **3 focused modules**.

### Module 1 — Understanding Social Engineering

Learn how manipulation and psychological pressure are used in scams.

### Module 2 — Recognizing Digital Scams

Identify suspicious messages, links, offers, and requests.

### Module 3 — Protecting Yourself and Others


# 28. Ethical Design

REWIRE is explicitly a **fraud-prevention platform**.

It must never become a training ground for fraud execution.

Therefore:

### REWIRE teaches:

* recognition,
* prevention,
* critical thinking,
* victim protection,
* reporting,
* digital safety,
* defensive analysis.

### REWIRE does not teach:

* how to deceive people,
* how to steal credentials,
* how to bypass security,
* how to execute financial fraud,
* or how to evade law enforcement.

The simulation environment should use **controlled fictional scenarios** designed to test recognition and decision-making.

# 32. The 2–3 Minute Pitch

Nigeria is building one of Africa's fastest-growing digital economies.

But there is a problem.

As more young people move online, fraud becomes more sophisticated—and for some young people, online fraud can appear to be one of the few easily accessible ways to monetize digital skills.

Most anti-fraud solutions focus on the victim.

We asked a different question:

**What if we could redirect the skills and curiosity surrounding fraud toward preventing it?**

That is REWIRE.

REWIRE is an anti-fraud EdTech and opportunity platform inspired by the Hong Kong Anti-Deception Coordination Centre's online learning model.

Hong Kong has built an interactive anti-scam education system around real-world cases, videos, quizzes and certification.

We take that principle and localize it for Nigeria.

A learner joins REWIRE and goes through practical anti-fraud lessons.

But we don't stop at education.

They enter realistic, controlled scenarios and have to identify suspicious behaviour, recognize manipulation, and make decisions.

Their performance creates a measurable Fraud Prevention Skill Profile.

Once they demonstrate sufficient competency, they receive a verified REWIRE credential.

And then comes the most important part.

**Opportunity.**

Instead of ending with "congratulations, you completed a course," REWIRE connects those skills to legitimate pathways in digital safety, trust and safety, cybersecurity, fraud prevention, internships, research, and other digital opportunities.

So the transformation becomes:

**Learn. Prove. Earn. Protect.**

We are not building a platform that teaches people how to commit fraud.

We are building a platform that takes an understanding of fraud and redirects it toward prevention and legitimate economic value.

Our vision is simple:

**What if the people who understand fraud best could become the people who stop it?**

That's REWIRE.

**Turning fraud exposure into fraud prevention, skills, and opportunity.**
